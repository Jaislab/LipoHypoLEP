# FIGURE 2 | What does leptin treatment change in LD mice?

**Entirely synthetic small-effect, low-variance demonstration. No experimental measurements or biological conclusions are shown.** The comparison is **LD LEPTIN vs LD SALINE**, with six simulated mice per group. All mice are on the LDLR−/− background. Positive log2 fold change indicates higher expression in LD LEPTIN. The simulation is not fitted to brain tissue and is not a prediction of biological variability, experimental effects, or power. The intended effect scale around ±0.2 log2 units is not a cutoff or an axis boundary; fitted estimates can exceed it.

**a,** Volcano plot of 8,000 retained synthetic genes. Coral and blue identify higher and lower expression in LD LEPTIN, respectively, at adjusted *P* < 0.05; other genes are gray. There is no fold-change threshold. The dashed horizontal line marks adjusted *P* = 0.05, and the vertical line at zero marks the change in effect direction. In this simulation, 210 genes have adjusted *P* < 0.05: 96 higher and 114 lower. These counts are illustrative, not study results or power predictions.

**b,** Expression heatmap of the 40 genes with the smallest adjusted *P* values among all tested genes, restricted to the 12 LD LEPTIN and LD SALINE samples. Selection does not require significance or a minimum effect. Rows show gene-wise z-scores of log2-normalized expression across these samples (sample standard deviation, ddof = 1) and are ordered by average-linkage Euclidean clustering. The displayed scale is limited to −2 to 2. Row standardization shows expression patterns rather than absolute effect magnitudes. Columns are individual mice, grouped by the full study-group names.

**c,** Estimated effects for the six genes with the smallest adjusted *P* values in each direction among all tested genes, sorted by effect, without requiring significance or a minimum effect size. Intervals are marginal, unadjusted 95% confidence intervals from the common fitted model (coefficient ± *t*<sub>0.975,15</sub> × standard error). Gene selection uses the same data; these intervals do not provide multiplicity- or selection-adjusted coverage for the displayed set. SIM labels are anonymous synthetic identifiers.

This contrast illustrates treatment-associated differences within LD. It does not by itself establish that treatment reverses an LD-associated difference relative to NON-LD control.
